# Iran Service-Product Demand Methodology

Snapshot date: **2026-10-02**

This document explains how the service-product opportunity library estimates and sorts demand in Iran.

## What is being ranked

The ranking is about **demand for the underlying service in Iran**, not only the current popularity of online apps for that service.

A service can therefore have:

- high underlying demand but low digital maturity;
- moderate underlying demand but a strong online marketplace;
- little validated Iranian demand even though the model is proven internationally.

The library keeps **Iran demand** and **digital maturity** as separate fields.

## Demand tiers

- **D5 — Very high / mass-market:** broad or frequent demand across major Iranian cities; large addressable service volume or an already mainstream digital category.
- **D4 — High:** strong and recurring demand with broad urban relevance, but less universal or frequent than D5.
- **D3 — Medium / established:** clearly real Iranian demand with meaningful providers/customers, usually more segment-specific.
- **D2 — Low / niche:** valid Iranian market, but narrower geography, audience, frequency, or willingness to transact digitally.
- **D1 — Very low / emerging:** small, fragmented, premium, culturally constrained, or early-market demand.
- **D0 — No validated local demand:** globally valid service-product model, but no meaningful Iranian demand was validated in this research snapshot. It remains in the library for portfolio completeness and future market changes.

Rows are sorted from D5 to D0. Ordering **inside a tier is approximate**, because comparable public transaction-volume data does not exist across all service categories.

## Digital-maturity levels

- **M5 — Mainstream digital:** major established platforms and normal online transaction behavior.
- **M4 — Strong digital market:** several active platforms or strong online booking/marketplace behavior.
- **M3 — Emerging/credible:** working digital products exist, but the market remains fragmented or partly offline.
- **M2 — Limited digital:** some products/listings exist; most fulfillment or discovery remains offline/manual.
- **M1 — Mostly offline:** very little productized digital behavior was found.
- **M0 — No meaningful local digital model validated.**

Digital maturity is a research aid, **not** a project-selection score. A high-demand/low-maturity category can be especially interesting for a portfolio project.

## Evidence signals

Demand tiers are estimated from multiple signals rather than a single metric:

1. Presence and breadth of active Iranian marketplaces or booking products.
2. Number and breadth of service categories exposed by large local service platforms.
3. Evidence of repeated/recurring consumer need and nationwide or multi-city availability.
4. Visible provider density, booking inventory, marketplace depth, or transaction/review signals when public.
5. Regulatory/tariff evidence for formal service categories such as home nursing.
6. Presence of multiple specialized products in a vertical.
7. Offline market prevalence when online maturity is still weak.

Do **not** interpret the tiers as audited market-size estimates or investment advice.

The snapshot uses public market evidence including:

- HomeServize service catalog and pricing: https://homeservize.com/ and https://m.homeservize.com/service — more than 500 services across major groups, including cleaning, building trades, appliance repair, auto service, beauty, healthcare/nursing, pet and education services.

## Model-variant rule

Each product family lists several **common product models**. These are not features. They are materially different ways the service business can be structured.

Variants can differ by:

- provider structure: single provider, chain, independent professional, marketplace, managed marketplace;
- fulfillment location: fixed location, customer location, remote/online, hybrid;
- transaction type: instant booking, request-to-book, quote-first, dispatch, subscription, package/credits;
- audience: consumer, B2B, family/caregiver, gender-specific, premium, corporate;
- operational depth: appointment-only, lifecycle tracking, multi-actor coordination, resource scheduling.

Before Phase 01 begins, the user must select **both the product family and the product model/variant**. The selected model becomes part of the Selected Opportunity Brief and remains an explicit product boundary unless deliberately changed later.
