# Problem Definition — Women’s Beauty Salon Booking

## Context

Women seeking beauty services often move from a service need to a confirmed appointment through calls, direct messages, WhatsApp, or other manual coordination with a salon. Information about services, specialists, pricing, duration, and availability is frequently fragmented across social posts, messages, staff knowledge, and calendars.

## Target users

### Customer
A woman who wants to understand a beauty service, choose an appropriate specialist, find a suitable time, and secure or manage an appointment with confidence.

### Salon staff
Reception or management staff who repeatedly answer booking questions, match services to specialists, check schedules, and record appointment changes.

## Current behavior

A typical customer journey today is:

1. Decide she needs a beauty service.
2. Find or return to a salon.
3. Review social media, portfolios, or informal information.
4. Contact the salon to ask about the service, price, duration, specialist, and availability.
5. Exchange several messages or calls to find a workable option.
6. Have the salon record the appointment manually.
7. Contact the salon again if the appointment must change.

## Primary friction

### Customer-side friction

- Service information is not always complete or structured.
- Specialist suitability is difficult to evaluate consistently.
- Pricing and duration may be unclear before contact.
- Real bookable availability is not visible.
- Booking often requires back-and-forth communication.
- Rescheduling or cancellation often requires another manual conversation.

### Salon-side friction

Staff must repeatedly combine:

- selected service
- specialist eligibility
- service duration
- specialist working schedule
- existing appointments
- customer preference

into one valid appointment. This turns reception into a manual coordination layer and increases operational effort as demand and staff count grow.

## Triggers

Common triggers include:

- routine recurring beauty maintenance
- an upcoming event or occasion
- an urgent beauty need before a deadline
- seeing a desired style or result
- reaching the expected time for a repeat visit

Some journeys are therefore time-sensitive, not merely convenience-driven.

## Desired outcomes

### Customer

Understand the available options, choose an appropriate service and specialist, know the expected price/duration, find a valid time, and reach a confirmed appointment without unnecessary coordination.

After booking, the customer should clearly understand what was booked, with whom, and when, and should be able to manage the appointment when policy allows.

### Salon

Allow standard bookings to happen with less manual intervention while preserving service eligibility, schedules, duration requirements, and salon booking rules.

## Initial constraints

- Not every specialist provides every service.
- Service durations vary.
- A time is bookable only if the complete service duration fits the relevant specialist schedule.
- Some services may later require additional intake or consultation context.
- Specialist trust, style, and expertise can materially affect the customer decision.

## Assumptions

These are working assumptions, not validated research findings:

1. Customers are willing to complete standard booking steps through self-service.
2. Visible valid availability reduces booking friction.
3. Specialist choice matters for a meaningful subset of services.
4. Pricing and duration clarity influence confidence and conversion.
5. A meaningful share of reception workload comes from predictable booking coordination.

## Root causes

1. Service, specialist, pricing, duration, and availability information is fragmented.
2. Availability is usually hidden inside staff knowledge or an internal calendar.
3. Booking rules depend on staff knowledge rather than an explicit customer-facing system.
4. Specialist choice includes trust and fit, not only schedule availability.
5. Messaging and phone calls are being used as discovery, consultation, availability-check, and booking tools at the same time.

## Consequences

### For customers

- uncertainty before booking
- more time spent coordinating
- increased chance of misunderstanding price, duration, or specialist fit
- dependence on salon response speed
- repeated effort for simple appointment changes

### For the salon

- repetitive staff workload
- higher risk of scheduling mistakes or misunderstandings
- customer experience depends heavily on reception responsiveness
- operational complexity grows with more customers, services, and specialists

## Open questions

- Are all services directly bookable?
- Which services require consultation or additional intake?
- Which services have fixed, ranged, or quote-based pricing?
- Can customers choose any eligible specialist automatically?
- What cancellation and rescheduling rules apply?
- Do any services require resources beyond a specialist?
- Is guest booking allowed or is an account required?
- Will the initial product support only one salon location?
- Is payment handled at the salon or partly online?

## Final problem statement

Women seeking beauty services struggle to confidently find and arrange the right appointment because service, specialist, pricing, duration, and availability information are fragmented and booking depends heavily on manual salon coordination.

The product should reduce uncertainty and unnecessary coordination between “I need this beauty service” and “my valid appointment is confirmed,” while preserving the salon’s operational control over specialists, schedules, and booking rules.
